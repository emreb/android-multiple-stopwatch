Positioning

MultiLaps is a lap timer for the person standing at the trackside with more than one runner to watch. It puts four stopwatches on one screen, each in its runner's colour, so nobody's lap gets missed while you fumble between timers.

Best forA parent or volunteer coach timing two to four children by hand at a running track, where the runners set off at different moments and each lap matters to them. Also swimming lengths, cycling laps and school sports days.
Not for: races with timing chips, groups larger than four, or anyone who needs results as a spreadsheet.
CategoryA multi-runner lap stopwatch for Android. People will look for it as "multiple stopwatch" or "lap timer".
What people use now
  • The phone's built-in clock app, which times one runner. The second child gets timed by guesswork.
  • Two phones, or a phone and a wristwatch, one in each hand.
  • A handheld sports stopwatch, one per runner, and a clipboard.
  • General "multi timer" apps built for cooking or workouts: long lists, small buttons, adverts between you and the Lap button.
Why they fall shortThey are built for one thing being timed, or for timers you set and walk away from. At the track you are watching children, not a screen, and you have about a second to press the right button for the right child.
What is different
  • Four at once, always visible. No list to scroll and no switching. Turn the phone on its side and they sit in a row.
  • Found by colour. Each stopwatch is a solid block in its runner's colour, with their name on it.
  • Built around the Lap press. Lap and Stop are big buttons, Lap solid and Stop an outline. A double press is ignored instead of recording a bogus lap.
  • Hard to lose a time. A reset can be undone for five seconds. Timing carries on if the screen locks, a call comes in or the app is closed.
  • Nothing in the way. No adverts, no account, no internet connection.
Proof you can state today
  • The app asks for one permission: to vibrate. It cannot reach the internet.
  • Every behaviour above is covered by an automated test; the suite has 387.
  • Text meets the contrast level set for small print on all eight colours.
There are no user reviews or usage numbers yet. Do not invent any.
Positioning in one sentence

For parents and coaches who time several runners by hand, MultiLaps is a lap stopwatch that shows four runners at once, each in their own colour. Unlike the clock app on your phone, it never makes you choose which child to time.

Say what it does in the words a parent at a track would use. The product is simple; the copy should be too.

Taglines

Alternative, for parents

Alternative, the problem

Three things to say, in this order

1. Everyone at onceFour stopwatches side by side. Start each runner when they actually set off, or start them all together.
2. Laps without lookingOne big Lap button per runner, a different vibration for lap and stop, and each runner's current, last and best lap on their tile.
3. Nothing gets lostUndo a reset. Lock the screen, take a call, close the app: the timing is still running when you come back.

Voice

Do

  • Write the way one parent tells another about a useful app.
  • Use the track's words: lap, runner, start, stop, best lap.
  • Be specific. "Four runners", "five seconds to undo", "one permission".
  • Say what it does not do, plainly. It builds trust.

Avoid

  • Performance talk: "elite", "pro", "train smarter", "unlock your potential". This is for children having a run.
  • Calling it accurate to a hundredth. It shows hundredths, but a thumb decides the time.
  • Exclamation marks, and any claim about how many people use it.
  • "Timer" for the product. A timer counts down; this is a stopwatch.

MultiLaps puts four stopwatches on one screen, so every runner gets their own.

Everyone at once
Four stopwatches, each a solid block of colour with the runner's name on it. Start each one when that runner sets off, or press Start all when they go together. Turn the phone on its side and they line up in a row.

Made for the Lap press
Lap and Stop are two big buttons at the foot of every stopwatch. Lap is solid and Stop is an outline, so you can tell them apart at a glance. Each press gives a short vibration, different for a lap and a stop, so you can keep your eyes on the track. A double press is ignored instead of recording a lap that never happened.

The numbers that matter
Each stopwatch shows the lap in progress in large digits, with the last lap, best lap, total time and lap count beneath. Tap a name to see every lap, with the best one marked.

Hard to lose a time
Reset by mistake? Undo it within five seconds. Screen locked, call came in, app closed? The stopwatches are still running when you come back. The screen stays on while anything is being timed.

Look back later
Reset a stopwatch and its laps are kept in History, by day. Open a result to see every lap. Share one result as text, or pick several and share them together. Results stay on your phone until you delete them.

Yours to set up
Name each stopwatch and pick its colour from eight. They stay as you set them. Light and dark themes follow your phone.

Nothing in the way
No ads. No account. No internet connection: the app cannot go online, and your times stay on your phone unless you choose to share them.

Questions and facts

Answers to what people will ask before they install, and the facts to check any copy against.

Questions people will ask

Can I time more than four?No. Four is what fits on a phone screen with buttons big enough to press in a hurry.
Do they all have to start together?No. Each stopwatch starts on its own. Start all is there for when they do go together; it starts every stopwatch that has not been started and leaves the rest alone.
What if I press the wrong thing?A second press within a second is ignored. A reset, or Reset all, can be undone for five seconds.
What if my phone locks or the app closes?The stopwatches keep running. They also carry on through a phone restart, to the nearest the phone's own clock allows.
Does it save past sessions?Yes. When you reset a stopwatch that has at least one lap, what it timed is kept in History, by day, until you delete it.
Can I send results to someone?Yes, as text. Share one result from its page, or press and hold to pick several and share them together, through whichever app you choose.
Does it need the internet or an account?No. The app cannot go online and there is nothing to sign in to.
Why does it ask to vibrate?So you can feel each press. It is the only permission the app asks for.
I felt no vibration.The buzz follows the phone's main vibration switch and its alarm vibration level. It is also silenced by Do Not Disturb when that is set to total silence.

Before you publish

What this kit does not settle. The copy is written to be true of the app as it stands; these are the places where a claim needs checking or a piece is still missing.

Claims to confirm on a real phone

1The release build. The first signed build, 0.1.0, has been installed from Google Play and run on one phone. The 0.3.2 build, with History, has been run as a signed, optimised build on emulators only, Android 6.0 among them; its first run on a phone will be in testing.
2Sunlight. The contrast is measured, but nobody has yet read the screen at arm's length in sunlight. No line in this kit claims it; do not add one untested.
3"Carries on through a restart." Covered by tests only; never tried by restarting a phone mid-timing. The copy says "to the nearest the phone's own clock allows" for that reason.
4Android 9 and 10. On those versions a phone with touch feedback off will not vibrate. The listing does not promise otherwise, but a reviewer may notice.

Decisions the copy is waiting on

1Price. No line in this kit says "free". Add it once decided; it belongs in the short description.
2Publisher name and contact email, both required by Google Play.
3The package name com.swetw.multilaps is permanent once published. Convention expects you to control swetw.com.
4Privacy policy. Written, as PRIVACY.md at the top of the repository. It still needs the publisher name and contact email from decision 2.

Assets

1Made, in docs/play-store/: the feature graphic at 1024 × 500 px, the store icon at 512 × 512 px, and seven captioned screenshots.
2Still to make, optional: a short video. Fifteen seconds of four runners being lapped would show the idea faster than any caption.

What not to say

  • No testimonials, ratings, download counts or "trusted by" lines until they exist.
  • Nothing about exporting files, spreadsheets or backing up. Results are shared as text and are not backed up.
  • No claim of precision beyond "shows hundredths". The time is taken by hand.
